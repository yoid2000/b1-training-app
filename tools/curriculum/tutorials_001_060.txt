# Unit | pattern | rules separated by ~ | German => English examples | reminder
001|subject + haben + … + past participle|Conjugate haben for the subject: habe, hast, hat, haben, habt, haben. The participle normally closes the main clause.~Regular participles usually have ge- + stem + -t; stems ending in -t or -d often need -et. In a question, move the auxiliary, not the participle.|Wir haben die Unterlagen geprüft. => We checked the documents.~Hast du lange gewartet? => Did you wait a long time?|A past-time expression does not replace the auxiliary: gestern habe ich gearbeitet.
002|subject + sein + … + past participle|Use sein with many intransitive changes of place or state, and with bleiben. Conjugate bin, bist, ist, sind, seid, sind.~Movement alone is not a universal rule: the verb's meaning and use decide the auxiliary. Learn the auxiliary with the verb; actions such as reading still take haben during a journey.|Die Gäste sind früh angekommen. => The guests arrived early.~Ich bin zu Hause geblieben. => I stayed at home.|Contrast moving home, ist umgezogen, with changing clothes, hat sich umgezogen.
003|strong: ge- + changed stem + -en; mixed: changed stem + -t|Strong verbs often change their vowel and use -en: schreiben → geschrieben, trinken → getrunken. Their forms must be learned individually.~Mixed verbs combine a stem change with -t: bringen → gebracht, denken → gedacht. The participle does not change with the subject, but haben/sein does.|Sie hat einen Bericht geschrieben. => She wrote a report.~Wir haben an den Termin gedacht. => We thought about the appointment.|Do not form a regular participle just because the infinitive looks familiar; learn principal parts together.
004|aufmachen → aufgemacht; bezahlen → bezahlt|With ordinary separable prefixes, ge- goes between prefix and stem: abholen → abgeholt.~Inseparable be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver- and zer- do not add ge-. The base verb also matters: vorbereiten → vorbereitet and ausprobieren → ausprobiert have no added ge-.|Er hat die Tür zugemacht. => He closed the door.~Sie hat die Rechnung bezahlt. => She paid the bill.|Identify both prefix type and base verb before inserting ge-.
005|-ieren → -iert, without ge-|Verbs with the productive ending -ieren form their participle with -iert: organisieren → organisiert. Most use haben, but the auxiliary is still learned separately.~Do not apply this spelling shortcut to every similar-looking ending. Frieren has gefroren; feiern has gefeiert. Passieren meaning happen uses sein.|Wir haben die Reise organisiert. => We organized the trip.~Was ist passiert? => What happened?|A separable prefix does not restore ge- to an -ieren base: ausprobiert.
006|sein: war …; haben: hatte …|For sein, learn war, warst, war, waren, wart, waren in the order ich, du, er/sie/es, wir, ihr, sie/Sie.~For haben, use hatte, hattest, hatte, hatten, hattet, hatten. These simple past forms describe past states naturally; they do not need a second auxiliary or a participle.|Früher waren wir Nachbarn. => We used to be neighbors.~Hattet ihr damals einen Garten? => Did you have a garden then?|Formal Sie always uses waren or hatten, never wart or hattet.
007|past modal + … + bare infinitive|The past stems are konnt-, musst-, durft-, sollt-, wollt- and mocht-, without an umlaut. Add -e, -est, -e, -en, -et, -en.~Conjugate only the modal and put the other verb's infinitive at the end. Können and mögen have different hypothetical forms: könnte and möchte are not the factual simple past.|Wir mussten den Bus nehmen. => We had to take the bus.~Du konntest schon gut schwimmen. => You could already swim well.|Mochte describes past liking; möchte normally expresses a present wish.
008|kommen → kam; sagen → sagte|Kommen has a strong past stem: kam, kamst, kam, kamen, kamt, kamen. Sagen is regular: sagte, sagtest, sagte, sagten, sagtet, sagten.~Use these finite past verbs in ordinary verb-second main clauses. If a time expression comes first, the subject follows the verb; no haben or sein is added.|Gestern kam der Besuch spät. => Yesterday the visitors arrived late.~Wir sagten sofort Bescheid. => We let them know immediately.|Kamt belongs to ihr; kamen belongs to wir, sie and formal Sie.
009|dative articles: dem / der / dem / den|A verb or preposition determines whether a noun phrase needs dative. The definite forms are dem for masculine/neuter, der for feminine and den for plural.~Plural nouns usually add -n in dative: Freunde → Freunden. Do not add another ending to plurals already ending in -n or -s. The indefinite forms are einem and einer.|Ich fahre mit der Fähre. => I travel by ferry.~Sie spricht mit den Freunden. => She is speaking with the friends.|Check both the determiner and the plural noun: den Kindern, but den Autos.
010|gefallen / folgen / fehlen / zuhören + dative|Some verbs select a dative complement without a preposition: helfen, danken, folgen, begegnen, vertrauen and zuhören. Learn this case with each verb.~With gefallen, fehlen and gehören, distinguish the subject from the dative person. In Das Buch gefällt dem Kind, the book controls gefällt; the child experiences the liking.|Die Erklärung hilft den Gästen. => The explanation helps the guests.~Der Schlüssel gehört der Nachbarin. => The key belongs to the neighbor.|A person is not automatically dative; the particular verb determines the case.
011|ich→mir; du→dir; er→ihm; sie→ihr; wir→uns; ihr→euch; sie→ihnen; Sie→Ihnen|Replace a dative noun phrase with the corresponding dative personal pronoun. The pronoun keeps the case required by the verb or preposition.~Use ihr for a singular female person, ihnen for a third-person plural group and capitalized Ihnen for formal address. Uns and euch have the same forms in accusative and dative.|Ich helfe ihm. => I am helping him.~Kann ich mit Ihnen sprechen? => May I speak with you? (formal)|Do not choose a pronoun from English word order; first identify its German case.
012|person receiving: dative; thing transferred: accusative|Geben, schicken, zeigen, erklären and similar verbs can take two objects. Ask who receives or benefits, then what is given, shown or explained.~The recipient normally takes dative; the transferred content takes accusative. Masculine singular articles make the distinction especially visible: dem Kunden versus den Vertrag.|Sie zeigt dem Gast den Garten. => She shows the guest the garden.~Ich schicke meiner Schwester ein Paket. => I am sending my sister a parcel.|Both objects have their own case; one is not determined by the other object's position.
013|two nouns: dative–accusative; two unstressed pronouns: accusative–dative|With two full noun objects, neutral order usually places the dative recipient before the accusative thing.~A personal pronoun normally precedes a full noun object. With two unstressed personal pronouns, put the accusative pronoun first: es ihm, sie dir. Contrastive stress can change normal information order.|Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch. => I give the child the book.~Ich gebe es ihm. => I give it to him.|Treat these as neutral-order patterns, not a claim that every differently stressed order is impossible.
014|dative possessives: meinem / meiner / meinem / meinen|A possessive determiner identifies an owner, but its ending follows the gender, number and case of the possessed noun.~Use -em for masculine/neuter dative, -er for feminine and -en for plural. Unser becomes unserem/unserer/unseren; euer usually loses the second e: eurem, eurer, euren. Formal Ihr remains capitalized.|Wir fahren mit unserem Wagen. => We travel in our car.~Sie hilft ihrer Schwester. => She helps her sister.|In seinem Auto, the ending follows das Auto, not the owner's gender.
015|accusative reflexives: mich, dich, sich, uns, euch, sich|Use a reflexive pronoun when the verb's object refers back to its subject. Common frames include sich beeilen, sich erinnern and sich beschweren.~Without another accusative object, verbs such as sich waschen and sich anziehen use accusative reflexives. Third-person singular, plural and formal Sie all use sich; the verb's other required prepositions remain.|Ich beeile mich. => I am hurrying.~Ihr bereitet euch auf den Kurs vor. => You are preparing for the course. (plural)|Match the reflexive pronoun to the subject even when the reflexive verb is an infinitive after a modal.
016|ich wasche mich ↔ ich wasche mir die Hände|When the action already has an accusative object, a reflexive beneficiary can be dative. Only first- and second-person singular visibly differ: mir/dir rather than mich/dich.~Third-person sich, plural uns and euch stay the same in both cases. Learn the whole meaning frame: sich vorstellen can mean introduce oneself, while sich etwas vorstellen means imagine something.|Du putzt dir die Zähne. => You brush your teeth.~Ich stelle mir eine Reise vor. => I imagine a trip.|A second object does not make every reflexive verb dative automatically; learn the verb's construction.
017|einander; mit+einander → miteinander|Einander explicitly means each other, so it requires a group capable of mutual actions. It has one unchanged form for the cases governed by the verb.~Combine a required preposition with einander: aufeinander warten, füreinander sorgen, voneinander lernen. Reflexive sich alone can be ambiguous between oneself and each other; einander makes reciprocity clearer.|Die Freunde helfen einander. => The friends help each other.~Wir sprechen miteinander. => We speak with each other.|Do not substitute miteinander for every reciprocal expression; retain the preposition required by the verb.
018|location: dative; destination: accusative|The two-way prepositions are an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor and zwischen. Use dative to locate an activity or object; use accusative for the destination of a change of position.~Physical movement is not enough to trigger accusative. Walking within a park still answers where; entering the park answers where to.|Die Tasche liegt unter dem Tisch. => The bag is under the table.~Ich lege sie unter den Tisch. => I put it under the table.|Compare im Park laufen with in den Park laufen; the destination, not movement alone, decides.
019|position: liegen/stehen/sitzen; placement: legen/stellen/setzen|Liegen, stehen and sitzen describe an existing position; legen, stellen and setzen describe putting someone or something into a position.~With a two-way preposition, position uses dative and placement uses accusative. Learn the participle pairs gelegen/gelegt, gestanden/gestellt and gesessen/gesetzt. Hängen also contrasts a position, gehangen, with transitive placement, gehängt.|Das Glas steht auf dem Tisch. => The glass is standing on the table.~Ich stelle es auf den Tisch. => I put it on the table.|Keep regional haben/sein choices for position verbs separate from the position-versus-placement distinction.
020|her-: toward the speaker; hin-: away|Choose direction relative to the speaker or adopted viewpoint: herein/hinein for inward, heraus/hinaus for outward, herauf/hinauf for upward and herunter/hinunter for downward movement.~Use hierher for toward here and dorthin for toward there. Everyday rein, raus, rauf and runter often leave the her/hin distinction unspecified; context supplies the viewpoint.|Ich bin drinnen. Komm herein! => I am inside. Come in toward me!~Ich bleibe unten. Geh hinauf! => I am staying downstairs. Go upstairs away from me!|State where the speaker is before deciding between the full her- and hin- forms.
021|durch, für, gegen, ohne, um, bis + accusative|These prepositions govern accusative, independently of their meanings: route, purpose, opposition, absence, surrounding position or an endpoint.~Use den/einen for masculine singular, die/eine for feminine and das/ein for neuter. Bis often precedes an article-free time/place expression. With an added preposition, follow that preposition's case: bis zum Bahnhof.|Wir laufen durch den Wald. => We walk through the forest.~Das Geschenk ist für einen Freund. => The gift is for a friend.|Bis plus zu does not keep accusative: zum is zu dem, a dative phrase.
022|aus, außer, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu, gegenüber + dative|These prepositions require dative in their ordinary frames: dem/einem for masculine/neuter, der/einer for feminine and den plus the appropriate plural noun.~Gegenüber can stand before or after its complement. Some preposition–article combinations contract: beim, vom, zum, zur. A destination after zu still takes dative because zu is not a two-way preposition.|Sie kommt aus einem Dorf. => She comes from a village.~Das Hotel liegt dem Bahnhof gegenüber. => The hotel is opposite the station.|Do not give every destination accusative; the chosen preposition controls the case.
023|seit: continuing; vor: ago; in: from now|Seit plus dative gives the beginning or length of a state continuing into the reference time, often with present tense in German.~Vor plus dative locates an event a stated distance in the past; in plus dative can locate an event a stated distance in the future. Distinguish the event of moving in from the continuing state of living there.|Ich lerne seit einem Jahr Deutsch. => I have been learning German for a year.~Wir fahren in zwei Tagen ab. => We leave in two days.|German normally uses present tense for a state still continuing after seit, not an English-style perfect continuous.
024|für + duration; bis + end; von … bis; von … an|Für introduces an intended or agreed duration and takes accusative. Bis marks an endpoint, often with an article-free expression; bis zu adds a dative boundary phrase.~Von … bis names both ends of an interval. Ab and von … an emphasize when something starts. Seit differs by looking back over an ongoing interval rather than merely announcing a starting boundary.|Das Zimmer ist für drei Nächte gebucht. => The room is booked for three nights.~Vom nächsten Montag an gilt der neue Plan. => The new plan applies from next Monday onward.|For a stated endpoint with an article, learn the complete frame: bis zum Ende, not bis dem Ende.
025|während + genitive noun phrase|As a preposition, während means during and takes a noun phrase. Formal written German uses genitive: des Kurses, der Reise, des Gesprächs, der Ferien.~Masculine/neuter singular nouns usually add -(e)s after des. Feminine and plural nouns normally have no new genitive ending. A following finite clause instead belongs to the conjunction construction studied later.|Während des Vortrags bleibt es ruhig. => It stays quiet during the talk.~Während der Ferien ist die Schule geschlossen. => The school is closed during the holidays.|Distinguish während des Kurses from während ich lerne; only the second has a finite subordinate verb.
026|after definite articles: -e or -en|In nominative singular after der/die/das, adjectives take -e. Feminine and neuter accusative also take -e; masculine accusative takes -en.~Use -en for all dative forms and for plural after a definite article. The article already signals much of the case/gender information, so the adjective uses this weak pattern rather than strong endings.|Die freundliche Kollegin hilft uns. => The friendly colleague helps us.~Ich spreche mit dem freundlichen Kollegen. => I speak with the friendly colleague.|Read the whole phrase: den neuen Kollegen is accusative singular, while den neuen Kollegen can also be dative plural in a suitable context.
027|ein guter Mann; eine gute Frau; ein gutes Kind|After ein-type determiners, an adjective supplies a missing gender ending: nominative ein guter Mann and ein gutes Kind. Feminine eine already has an ending, so use gute.~Masculine accusative is einen guten Mann; neuter accusative remains ein gutes Kind. All dative forms take adjective -en. Plural after kein or a possessive also takes -en.|Sie sucht ein ruhiges Zimmer. => She is looking for a quiet room.~Wir wohnen in einer kleinen Wohnung. => We live in a small apartment.|Kein and possessive determiners follow the same mixed adjective pattern as ein.
028|without an article: guter / gute / gutes / guten / gutem|With no article, the adjective must show the case/gender information: nominative frischer Kaffee, frische Milch, frisches Brot, frische Äpfel.~Masculine accusative uses -en; feminine/neuter accusative keep -e/-es. Dative uses -em for masculine/neuter, -er for feminine and -en for plural. Plural nouns may also need dative -n.|Wir kaufen frisches Gemüse. => We buy fresh vegetables.~Sie kocht mit frischen Zutaten. => She cooks with fresh ingredients.|An article-free adjective does not automatically end in -e; first determine gender, number and case.
029|adjective + -er … als …|Most comparatives add -er: schnell → schneller. Some common short adjectives also change their vowel: alt → älter, groß → größer. Learn irregular besser, mehr and lieber separately.~Teuer becomes teurer; dunkel becomes dunkler; hoch becomes höher. A predicative comparative has no noun ending. Use als to introduce the unequal comparison, not wie.|Dieser Zug ist schneller als der Bus. => This train is faster than the bus.~Die Wohnung ist teurer als früher. => The apartment is more expensive than before.|The -er of a comparative is different from a masculine adjective ending: ein schneller Zug has both jobs to consider.
030|comparative/superlative stem + adjective ending|Before a noun, a comparative still needs an adjective ending: schnell-er-e, schnell-er-en. Superlative stems usually use -(e)st- and likewise take an ending: die günstigste Wohnung.~Predicative superlatives use am …sten instead. Inflect the entire attributive form for its article, case, gender and number. Remember irregular stems such as besser-/best- and höher-/höchst-.|Ich nehme den schnelleren Zug. => I take the faster train.~Das ist die günstigste Verbindung. => That is the cheapest connection.|Do not place am schnellsten directly before a noun as though it were an attributive adjective.
031|so / genauso / ebenso + adjective + wie|Use wie with equality: so groß wie, genauso teuer wie, ebenso schnell wie. The adjective stays in its basic form rather than a comparative.~Negated equality still uses wie: nicht so groß wie. Use als after a comparative such as größer or besser. In casual speech these patterns may mix, but keep the distinction in standard written exercises.|Das Zimmer ist genauso hell wie unseres. => The room is just as bright as ours.~Der Weg ist nicht so lang wie gestern. => The route is not as long as yesterday.|Choose the comparison structure first: equality uses wie; inequality with a comparative uses als.
032|weil + subject + … + finite verb|Weil gives the reason for the main event and introduces a subordinate clause with the conjugated verb last. In ordinary perfect groups, the participle precedes the final auxiliary.~With a modal, the lexical infinitive precedes the finite modal. If the weil-clause comes first, the main clause begins with its finite verb. Separate the clauses with a comma.|Ich bleibe hier, weil es regnet. => I am staying here because it is raining.~Weil ich arbeiten muss, komme ich später. => Because I have to work, I will come later.|Use final verb order after weil in standard writing, even if you hear main-clause order in conversation.
033|wenn + condition, finite main verb + subject …|Use wenn for a realistic condition. Its finite verb goes last; the main clause states the consequence.~If the condition comes first, the whole wenn-clause fills the main clause's first position, so the main verb immediately follows the comma. German can use present tense for realistic future conditions without a separate future auxiliary.|Wenn du Zeit hast, ruf mich an. => If you have time, call me.~Wir fahren mit, wenn Platz ist. => We will come along if there is room.|Do not insert the subject before the main verb after a fronted wenn-clause: Wenn …, fahren wir …
034|immer / jedes Mal, wenn …|Wenn introduces repeated occasions, including past habits, and temporal situations in the present or future. The finite verb closes its clause.~Wann instead asks at what time, directly or indirectly. For one completed past occasion or life period, use als rather than wenn. A repeated past event still takes wenn when the repetition is the point.|Immer wenn es regnet, nehme ich den Bus. => Whenever it rains, I take the bus.~Ich weiß nicht, wann er kommt. => I do not know when he is coming.|The English word when does not always correspond to the same German word; decide between recurrence, a past occasion and a time question.
035|verb of saying/thinking + dass … finite verb|Dass introduces the content of a statement, belief, feeling or knowledge. Put the finite verb at the end, with the subject normally near the start of the subordinate clause.~A comma separates the content clause. Dass is a conjunction, so it has no gender or case ending. Das is an article or pronoun and can refer to a noun; it cannot simply replace dass.|Sie sagt, dass der Kurs morgen beginnt. => She says that the course starts tomorrow.~Ich glaube, dass wir genug Zeit haben. => I think that we have enough time.|Distinguish the spelling in das Buch, das ich lese from ich weiß, dass du liest.
036|asking/checking + ob … finite verb|Use ob to embed a yes/no question: Ist das Büro offen? becomes … ob das Büro offen ist. Unlike a direct question, the subordinate clause does not begin with the finite verb.~Choose ob when the uncertainty is whether something is true. Choose dass for a statement presented as content, and a W-word when specific missing information is requested.|Kannst du sagen, ob noch Plätze frei sind? => Can you say whether there are places left?~Ich prüfe, ob die Rechnung stimmt. => I am checking whether the bill is correct.|Ob does not mean every English if; a realistic condition normally uses wenn or falls.
037|W-word + subject + … + finite verb|Keep the information question's W-word but change to subordinate order: Wann beginnt der Kurs? → … wann der Kurs beginnt.~Wer/was can themselves be the subject; other question words ask about place, direction, origin, reason or manner. Use wo, wohin and woher according to the spatial relation. The introductory main clause keeps its own verb order.|Ich weiß nicht, warum sie wartet. => I do not know why she is waiting.~Er fragt, woher der Lärm kommt. => He asks where the noise comes from.|Do not copy the direct question's inverted verb order inside the indirect question.
038|deshalb / deswegen / daher + finite verb + subject|These words connect a consequence to an earlier fact. They are sentence constituents, not subordinating conjunctions, so a following main clause retains verb-second order.~When the result adverb comes first, the conjugated verb follows it immediately. The adverb can also occur later in the middle field. Contrast weil, which sends its clause's verb to the end, and coordinating denn.|Der Zug fällt aus. Deshalb nehmen wir den Bus. => The train is canceled. Therefore we take the bus.~Ich bleibe deswegen zu Hause. => I am staying home for that reason.|Deshalb ich nehme … is not the standard main-clause pattern; write deshalb nehme ich …
039|reflexive verb + fixed preposition + required case|Learn a whole frame: sich interessieren für + accusative, sich erinnern an + accusative, sich beschäftigen mit + dative. The reflexive pronoun still agrees with the subject.~Some verbs have different frames for different meanings. Sich freuen auf anticipates something; sich freuen über responds positively to something. A free location phrase does not replace the verb's required complement.|Ich interessiere mich für Geschichte. => I am interested in history.~Wir bedanken uns für die Einladung. => We thank you for the invitation.|Choose both the meaning and the preposition; a translation of the isolated verb is not enough.
040|jemand / niemand; jemanden / niemanden; jemandem / niemandem|Jemand means an unspecified person and niemand excludes all people. Nominative subjects use jemand/niemand; explicit accusative and dative forms end in -en and -em.~Uninflected object forms also occur in standard usage, especially in some environments. When an exercise requests an explicit case ending, supply it. Etwas and nichts refer to things or content rather than people.|Hat jemand angerufen? => Has anyone called?~Ich habe niemandem davon erzählt. => I have told nobody about it.|Do not treat jemand and etwas as interchangeable; one refers to people and the other to things.
041|welch- replaces an already understood noun|For an identifying question, inflect welch- like dies-: welcher Mann, welche Tasche, welches Buch; dative welchem/welcher/welchen. The same forms can stand without the noun.~Indefinite welche can mean some with plurals, and corresponding singular forms replace mass nouns: Brot → welches, Milch → welche, Klebstoff → welchen. A singular count noun normally needs ein-type substitution instead.|Wir brauchen Stifte. Hast du welche? => We need pens. Do you have any?~Mit welchem fährst du? (Bus) => Which one are you taking? (bus)|Choose a form from the omitted noun's gender/number and the pronoun's case in its new sentence.
042|hätte / wäre / könnte + polite request or preference|Konjunktiv II softens a request or presents a tentative preference. Use ich hätte, du hättest, wir/Sie hätten; ich wäre, du wärst, wir/Sie wären.~Könnte takes a bare infinitive at the end, as in könnten Sie helfen. Match the form to the person and keep formal Sie capitalized. These forms need not describe past time.|Könnten Sie kurz warten? => Could you wait a moment?~Ich hätte gern einen Termin. => I would like an appointment.|Konnte describes factual past ability; könnte can express a polite possibility now.
043|ein Drittel / ein Viertel / die Hälfte + genitive group|Fractions describe equal parts of a whole: Hälfte is 1/2, Drittel 1/3 and Viertel 1/4. A following genitive group names the whole: die Hälfte der Gäste.~Quantity expressions can also precede a measure: ein Viertel Liter, ein halbes Kilo. Halber/halbe/halbes acts like an adjective and inflects. An ordinal such as der dritte describes rank, not one third.|Ein Drittel der Gruppe fehlt. => One third of the group is absent.~Ich brauche ein halbes Kilo Mehl. => I need half a kilogram of flour.|Do not read every Viertel as a clock-time expression; look at the quantity it modifies.
044|weekday/time + -s; täglich; werktags|Recurring weekdays and parts of the day often use adverbs ending in -s: montags, abends, vormittags. Write these adverbs lowercase inside a sentence.~Am Montag can identify a particular Monday; montags states a recurring pattern. Täglich means every day. Werktags refers to working days and can include Saturday, so do not automatically interpret it as Monday–Friday in notices.|Dienstags habe ich Unterricht. => I have lessons on Tuesdays.~Sie übt täglich. => She practices every day.|Adverbs do not take noun/adjective endings simply because a preposition or plural noun appears elsewhere in the sentence.
045|number + -mal; -mal pro Woche/Monat …|Einmal, zweimal, dreimal and larger -mal forms count occurrences. They do not specify an interval unless one is added.~Zweimal angerufen counts two completed calls; zweimal pro Woche gives a recurring frequency. Separate the number of actions from their duration. Standard compact counting adverbs are written together.|Ich habe dreimal geklingelt. => I rang the bell three times.~Wir treffen uns zweimal pro Woche. => We meet twice a week.|Zweimal does not mean for two hours or twice as large; those are different quantity relations.
046|number + -fach; -fach so groß|Multiplicative forms compare amounts or sizes: zweifach is double, dreifach triple. Attach the multiplicative form to the quantity or quality being compared.~Distinguish three times as much, dreifach so viel, from three separate occurrences, dreimal. Einfach can mean single or simple/easy; the numerical context determines which meaning is intended.|Die Menge ist dreifach so groß. => The quantity is three times as large.~Wir brauchen eine zweifache Ausfertigung. => We need a document in duplicate.|When -fach is an attributive adjective, it still needs a normal adjective ending.
047|erstens, zweitens, drittens …|Enumeration adverbs organize arguments or steps. Use erstens for the first point, zweitens for the second and drittens for the third.~Unlike ordinal adjectives before nouns, these adverbs have no case ending. If an enumeration adverb begins a main clause, the finite verb follows it, because the adverb occupies the first position. Keep the items structurally parallel.|Erstens ist der Weg kurz; zweitens ist er sicher. => First, the route is short; second, it is safe.~Drittens brauchen wir mehr Zeit. => Third, we need more time.|Der dritte Grund uses an ordinal adjective; drittens introduces a point without naming a noun.
048|ordinal stem + normal adjective ending|Many ordinals use -t- up to nineteen and -st- from twenty: vierte, zwanzigste. Learn the special stems erst-, dritt-, siebt- and acht-.~Add the ending required by the noun phrase: der dritte Vorschlag, mit dem dritten Vorschlag. Ordinals can describe ranks, floors and steps, not just dates. Written digit ordinals normally have a following dot: der 3. Vorschlag.|Sie wohnt im dritten Stock. => She lives on the third floor.~Wir prüfen den zwanzigsten Antrag. => We check the twentieth application.|The ordinal stem identifies the rank; its final adjective ending identifies the grammatical role.
049|masculine/neuter: des + noun -(e)s; feminine/plural: der|Genitive noun phrases can express possession, membership or part–whole relations. Identify the possessor or containing whole rather than assuming a person must be involved.~Regular masculine/neuter singular nouns usually take -(e)s; feminine and plural nouns normally do not add a genitive ending. Weak masculine nouns follow their own -(e)n pattern instead. Adjectives after des/der usually take -en.|Die Tür des Hauses ist offen. => The door of the house is open.~Die Namen der Teilnehmer stehen hier. => The participants' names are listed here.|Do not add -s to every genitive noun: der Frau and der Kinder already have their correct forms.
050|innerhalb / außerhalb / statt / anstatt + genitive|Innerhalb and außerhalb delimit a time or space: inside a period/boundary versus outside it. Statt/anstatt introduces a replacement.~Use genitive as the formal written model: innerhalb eines Monats, außerhalb der Stadt, statt des Originals. Regional and conversational dative variants occur, but do not switch the case randomly in a formal exercise. These noun phrases are not finite subordinate clauses.|Innerhalb einer Woche bekommen Sie Bescheid. => You will be informed within a week.~Statt des Originals sende ich eine Kopie. => I am sending a copy instead of the original.|A preposition plus noun is different from statt dass or statt … zu with a verbal clause.
051|der Kunde → den/dem/des Kunden|Weak masculine nouns usually add -(e)n outside nominative singular: Kunde, Kollege, Mensch, Student, Zeuge. The article and the noun both carry the required case pattern.~Do not apply weak declension to all masculine nouns. Learn a noun's paradigm individually. Name has a mixed pattern: den/dem Namen but des Namens; the plural is Namen.|Ich sehe den neuen Studenten. => I see the new student.~Sie spricht mit dem Zeugen. => She speaks with the witness.|In dem Kunden, -n is a singular weak-noun ending, not evidence that the noun is plural.
052|ein Bekannter; die Bekannte; etwas Neues|When an adjective stands as a noun, capitalize it but retain adjective declension. A person can be ein Bekannter, eine Bekannte or der Bekannte according to the determiner.~After etwas and nichts, neuter nominalized adjectives often take strong -es: etwas Wichtiges. Dative gives etwas Wichtigem. The ending still depends on the entire noun phrase, not on capitalization alone.|Eine Bekannte hat angerufen. => A female acquaintance called.~Ich habe etwas Neues gelernt. => I learned something new.|Compare eine bekannte Frau with eine Bekannte: only the nominalized adjective is capitalized.
053|demonstratives: der/die/das; den/dem/der/denen; dies-|A standalone demonstrative replaces a noun and points back to an understood referent, often with emphasis. Gender and number come from that referent; case comes from the pronoun's new clause.~Use dem for masculine/neuter dative and denen for dative plural. Dies- generally follows the determiner's endings even when no noun follows. Do not simply preserve the antecedent's case.|Der Mann wartet. Den kenne ich. => The man is waiting. I know that man.~Die Gäste sind da. Mit denen spreche ich. => The guests are here. I am speaking with them.|Den Mann in one sentence can become der or dem in another when its grammatical role changes.
054|mein Buch → meines; mein Mantel → meiner|An independent possessive pronoun replaces both the possessive determiner and its noun. It therefore needs an ending even where an attributive determiner would have none.~Use meiner for masculine nominative, meine for feminine/plural nominative or accusative and meines for neuter. Cases still change the form: meinen Mantel → meinen, mit meinem Rad → mit meinem. Informal meins is a common neuter alternative.|Ist das dein Buch? Nein, meines liegt hier. => Is that your book? No, mine is here.~Du nimmst deinen Wagen; ich nehme meinen. => You take your car; I take mine.|Keep the omitted noun in mind; a possessive pronoun does not have one fixed ending for all belongings.
055|derselbe / dieselbe / dasselbe; solcher; jener|Derselbe identifies the same individual object or person; the first part inflects like a definite article: denselben, demselben, derselben.~Solcher describes a type or kind rather than identity; jener points to a more distant or previously contrasted referent. All must match case, gender and number. Distinguish the very same item from a separate item of the same kind.|Wir fahren mit demselben Zug. => We are taking the very same train.~Solche Aufgaben kenne ich schon. => I already know tasks of that kind.|The same model of car need not be dasselbe Auto; it may be ein gleiches Auto of the same type.
056|jeder + singular; einige/mehrere + plural|Jeder selects singular nouns and agreement: jeder Teilnehmer bekommt. Inflect jeder by case and gender: jede, jedes, jeden, jedem.~Einige and mehrere refer to plural groups; dative forms take -en and may require a noun ending: mit einigen Gästen. Viele and wenige usually behave as plural quantifiers; viel/wenig with mass nouns can remain uninflected, as in wenig Geld.|Jede Teilnehmerin erhält ein Blatt. => Every female participant receives a sheet.~Ich spreche mit mehreren Kollegen. => I speak with several colleagues.|Check the head noun's number before choosing the verb; jeder is not followed by a plural verb just because the group is large.
057|irgend- + person / thing / place / time / manner|Irgend- makes a reference deliberately nonspecific: irgendjemand for a person, irgendetwas for a thing, irgendwo for a place, irgendwann for a time and irgendwie for a way.~Irgendein still inflects like ein: irgendeinen Stift, mit irgendeiner Person. Person pronouns can show case endings: irgendjemanden, irgendjemandem. Select the information type before the grammatical ending.|Irgendjemand hat meinen Stift genommen. => Someone or other took my pen.~Wir finden irgendwann eine Lösung. => We will find a solution sometime.|Irgendwo cannot answer a time question, and irgendwie does not identify a destination.
058|niemand / keiner / nichts; noch nicht ↔ nicht mehr|Niemand excludes people; nichts excludes things or content. Kein- negates noun phrases or stands for members of an understood group, and its pronoun forms inflect.~Gar nichts and überhaupt kein intensify the negative. Noch nicht means not yet, leaving later completion possible. Nicht mehr means no longer: something used to hold but has stopped. Keep negation with the meaning you intend.|Ich habe noch keinen Termin. => I do not have an appointment yet.~Sie arbeitet nicht mehr hier. => She no longer works here.|Not yet and no longer describe different timelines; they are not interchangeable ways to strengthen nicht.
059|noun + identifying prepositional/adverbial attribute|A phrase after a noun can identify which person or thing is meant: die Wohnung über der Apotheke, der Mann mit dem Hut. It belongs inside the expanded noun phrase.~The attribute's own preposition controls its case independently of the head noun's case. Contrast an event-level adverbial, which tells where or how an action happens. Some short time/place expressions require context to decide their attachment.|Die Wohnung über der Apotheke ist frei. => The apartment above the pharmacy is available.~Ich suche den Mann mit dem roten Hut. => I am looking for the man with the red hat.|First locate the head noun, then ask whether the following phrase describes that noun or the whole event.
060|head noun → article/adjective agreement and verb number|Long noun phrases can contain genitives, prepositional attributes and coordinated descriptions. Find the head noun before choosing the phrase's gender, number and main verb.~A nearby plural attribute does not make a singular head plural: die Liste der Gäste ist. Inside each embedded phrase, apply its own case endings. A dative plural head may require den, adjective -en and noun -n simultaneously.|Die Liste der Teilnehmer ist vollständig. => The list of participants is complete.~Mit den neuen Kollegen meiner Schwester arbeite ich gern. => I enjoy working with my sister's new colleagues.|Do not make the verb agree with the nearest noun if that noun belongs only to an attribute.
