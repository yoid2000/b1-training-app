061|degree: ziemlich/völlig; focus: nur/sogar/auch/erst|Degree words modify intensity: ziemlich schwierig, völlig leer, besonders wichtig. Focus particles select an alternative or highlight an expectation, so their position changes what is emphasized.~Nur excludes alternatives; sogar includes a surprising example; auch adds another case. Erst restricts a time, amount or stage relative to expectation: a late start or still-small progress. Interpret the whole context, not a fixed English translation.|Nur Anna hat bezahlt. => Only Anna has paid.~Sogar der Chef hilft mit. => Even the boss is helping.|Moving nur from before a person to before a time or object changes what is being restricted.
062|mindestens ≥ minimum; höchstens ≤ maximum|Mindestens allows the stated amount or more; höchstens allows the stated amount or less. Fast alle means nearly all, rather than every member without exception.~Partitive noun phrases name a subset: ein Teil der Gruppe, die Hälfte der Plätze. With a singular head such as Teil or Hälfte, singular agreement is the straightforward model; with plural heads, use plural agreement. Eine Menge expresses a large quantity, not an exact number.|Höchstens fünf Gäste dürfen hinein. => At most five guests may enter.~Die Hälfte der Plätze ist frei. => Half the places are available.|Do not confuse an upper limit with a lower limit just because both contain a number.
063|denken an / warten auf / sich bewerben um + accusative|Many verbs require a fixed preposition that is not simply a free place phrase. Learn the verb, its meaning, preposition and case together: denken an den Termin, warten auf die Antwort.~Other accusative frames include sich interessieren für, sich kümmern um, sprechen über and sich freuen auf/über. Some verbs allow different prepositions with different meanings; keep the intended frame when replacing its noun or forming a question.|Wir warten auf den Bescheid. => We are waiting for the decision notice.~Sie bewirbt sich um einen Ausbildungsplatz. => She is applying for a training position.|A preposition's location meaning is not enough to decide case in a fixed verb frame.
064|teilnehmen an / sprechen mit / bestehen aus + dative|Fixed verb frames can govern dative even when the same preposition takes accusative in another use: teilnehmen an einer Sitzung.~Learn sprechen mit/von, fragen nach, bestehen aus, abhängen von and sich beschäftigen mit as complete frames. Conjugate a separable verb normally while keeping its prepositional complement: nimmt an … teil. Use dative pronouns when the complement refers to a person.|Ich nehme an einem Seminar teil. => I am taking part in a seminar.~Die Gruppe besteht aus zwölf Personen. => The group consists of twelve people.|The case belongs to this particular frame; do not import the case from a different verb with the same preposition.
065|sich bei jemandem für etwas bedanken|Some verbs have two prepositional complements with different jobs: bei identifies the person receiving thanks, and für identifies the reason. Apply each preposition's case independently.~Similar patterns are mit jemandem über etwas sprechen, sich bei jemandem für etwas entschuldigen and sich bei einer Firma um eine Stelle bewerben. Keeping only one preposition may leave out important meaning, but the two complements do not have to use the same case.|Ich bedanke mich bei dir für die Hilfe. => I thank you for the help.~Sie spricht mit dem Chef über den Vertrag. => She is speaking with the boss about the contract.|Identify person, topic, reason and goal before deciding which phrase belongs to which preposition.
066|zufrieden mit + dative; stolz auf + accusative|Adjectives, like verbs, can require complements: zufrieden mit, interessiert an and überzeugt von take dative; stolz auf, dankbar für and verantwortlich für take accusative in these meanings.~Some adjectives take a direct dative rather than a preposition: dem Vater ähnlich. Rare genitive frames such as sich seiner Verantwortung bewusst sein are mainly recognition targets here. Learn each adjective with its construction instead of translating the preposition from English.|Wir sind mit dem Ergebnis zufrieden. => We are satisfied with the result.~Sie ist auf ihre Tochter stolz. => She is proud of her daughter.|The adjective's complement has its own case even if the adjective itself is a simple predicate without an ending.
067|preposition + wen/wem for a person|Keep the verb's or adjective's required preposition when asking about a person. Use wen after an accusative frame and wem after a dative frame.~Examples include auf wen warten, an wen denken, mit wem sprechen and von wem hören. A prepositional question group normally occupies the first position in a direct question and is followed by the finite verb. Wo-compounds normally ask about things or propositions instead.|Mit wem hast du gesprochen? => Who did you speak with?~Auf wen wartest du? => Who are you waiting for?|Wen and wem are not interchangeable; first identify the case governed by the frame.
068|da + preposition; add r before a vowel|Use da-compounds to refer to an understood thing or proposition: damit, dafür, davon, darauf, daran, darüber. Before vowel-initial an, auf, in or über, add r after da.~The verb keeps its required preposition inside the compound. For personal reference, normally retain a preposition plus personal pronoun instead: mit ihm, auf sie. A compound can also point forward to an explanatory clause rather than back to a noun.|Ich warte auf die Antwort. Ich warte darauf. => I am waiting for the answer. I am waiting for it.~Wir freuen uns darüber, dass du kommst. => We are pleased that you are coming.|Damit can be a da-compound meaning with it or a conjunction meaning so that; identify its role in the sentence.
069|wo + preposition; r before a vowel|Wo-compounds ask about a thing or proposition governed by a preposition: worauf, woran, worüber, womit, wofür. Use the preposition required by the verb, not one selected only from the object's meaning.~Add r before a vowel, but not before consonants: woran versus womit. For people, normally use preposition plus wen/wem instead. The compound forms one question constituent in a direct or indirect question.|Worüber sprecht ihr? => What are you talking about?~Ich weiß nicht, worauf sie wartet. => I do not know what she is waiting for.|Wofür asks about a thing/purpose; für wen asks about a person.
070|versuchen / hoffen / vergessen + zu-infinitive|Many verbs take an infinitive group with zu when the action's understood subject is supplied by the surrounding construction. Put objects and other complements before the infinitive.~With separable verbs, insert zu between prefix and stem: abzuschicken, anzurufen. An inseparable verb keeps zu separate: zu bezahlen. A clause-like expanded infinitive group is separated by a comma. Ordinary modal verbs instead take a bare infinitive.|Ich versuche, früher aufzustehen. => I am trying to get up earlier.~Sie hat vergessen, die Tür abzuschließen. => She forgot to lock the door.|Do not add zu after a modal simply because another verb appears later: ich muss gehen.
071|es ist wichtig/möglich/schön, … zu + infinitive|An adjective or impersonal evaluation can take an infinitive clause: es ist wichtig, etwas zu prüfen. Introductory es fills the subject role while the later group names the action being evaluated.~The understood person doing the action is supplied by context, not by the word es. Keep the infinitive group's own objects and reflexive pronouns, and separate an expanded clause-like group with a comma. A finite dass-clause can be clearer if an explicit different subject is needed.|Es ist wichtig, die Frist einzuhalten. => It is important to meet the deadline.~Es ist schön, dich wiederzusehen. => It is nice to see you again.|Do not conjugate the last verb of a zu-infinitive group as though it had an explicit subject.
072|noun + comma + … zu-infinitive|Nouns such as Absicht, Möglichkeit, Zeit and Lust can be explained by an infinitive group: the group says what intention, opportunity or activity is meant.~Use the ordinary zu forms, including internal zu in separable verbs. Keep a comma around a clause-like group; when it is embedded inside the larger sentence, close it with a second comma. Unlike um … zu, a noun complement does not automatically express the purpose of the main event.|Ich habe die Möglichkeit, im Ausland zu arbeiten. => I have the opportunity to work abroad.~Die Absicht, früher abzureisen, überrascht mich. => The intention to leave earlier surprises me.|Ask whether the infinitive explains a noun or gives an action's purpose before choosing its introductory structure.
073|subject clause + singular main predicate|A whole dass-, W- or infinitive clause can function as the subject. Treat that clausal unit as singular even if it mentions several people.~Keep each clause's own verb order: the subordinate finite verb closes its clause, while the main predicate remains outside. A clause-like infinitive subject is separated by a comma. Introductory es can stand before a later subject clause, but need not remain when that clause itself comes first.|Dass ihr alle helft, freut mich. => The fact that you all help pleases me.~Was sie erzählt, ist interessant. => What she tells us is interesting.|The main verb agrees with the whole subject clause, not with a plural noun inside it.
074|main action, ohne … zu + infinitive|Ohne … zu describes an accompanying action that does not happen. In the usual learner pattern, its understood actor is the same as the main action's subject.~Place the reflexive pronoun and objects inside the infinitive group; insert zu into separable verbs. Use a comma before ohne. If a different actor must be stated explicitly, use a finite ohne dass-clause instead; ohne plus a noun is a separate preposition frame.|Er ging, ohne sich zu verabschieden. => He left without saying goodbye.~Sie bezahlte, ohne die Rechnung zu prüfen. => She paid without checking the bill.|Do not supply a second subject inside an ordinary ohne … zu group.
075|main action, statt/anstatt … zu + infinitive|Statt … zu identifies an alternative action that is replaced by the main action. The ordinary infinitive pattern shares its understood actor with the main subject.~Put complements before the final infinitive and use a comma before statt/anstatt. If the alternative needs a different explicit subject, use statt dass. A noun replacement, such as statt des Autos, has no infinitive and follows prepositional case rules.|Sie fährt Rad, statt das Auto zu nehmen. => She cycles instead of taking the car.~Er sieht fern, statt den Bericht zu schreiben. => He watches television instead of writing the report.|The main action is the chosen course; the statt-group names the unused alternative.
076|lassen + object + bare infinitive|Lassen can mean allow someone to act or arrange for someone else to perform a service. It takes a bare infinitive, not zu: das Auto reparieren lassen.~With an adjective or state expression, it can mean leave unchanged: die Tür offen lassen. Learn lässt in third-person present and ließ in singular simple past. In perfect groups with another infinitive, use the replacement infinitive: hat reparieren lassen.|Ich lasse meine Haare schneiden. => I am having my hair cut.~Wir lassen die Kinder spielen. => We let the children play.|Arranging a repair does not mean the subject personally performs it; context decides the actor.
077|gehen/bleiben/sehen/hören + bare infinitive|Common constructions use an infinitive without zu: einkaufen gehen, sitzen bleiben, jemanden kommen sehen, jemanden singen hören. Learn the full lexical combination.~With perception verbs, the named person/object belongs semantically to the perceived action: ich höre ihn singen. Lernen can take a short bare infinitive such as schwimmen lernen, while expanded infinitive constructions can use zu. Do not extend the bare-infinitive pattern to every verb.|Sie bleibt am Fenster stehen. => She remains standing at the window.~Ich höre die Kinder lachen. => I hear the children laughing.|After a non-modal verb, the presence or absence of zu depends on the construction, not a universal rule.
078|nicht brauchen + zu-infinitive = no necessity|In the standard written model, brauchen with a negative or limiting expression takes zu plus infinitive. The meaning is do not need to, similar to nicht müssen.~With separable verbs, place zu inside the verb: auszufüllen. Keep objects and reflexive pronouns in the infinitive group. Nicht dürfen expresses prohibition instead; lack of necessity alone does not grant or deny permission.|Du brauchst heute nicht zu kommen. => You do not need to come today.~Ihr braucht nichts mitzubringen. => You do not need to bring anything.|Do not translate must not as nicht brauchen; a prohibition uses nicht dürfen.
079|müssen ≠ dürfen; sollen ≠ wollen; können = ability/possibility|Müssen expresses necessity; dürfen permission; können ability or possibility; wollen intention. Sollen can relay someone else's instruction or expectation rather than state the speaker's own wish.~Negative scope is especially important: nicht müssen removes a requirement, whereas nicht dürfen prohibits an action. Conjugate the modal and keep the lexical infinitive at the end. A doctor's or employer's instruction often appears with sollen in a report.|Wir sollen die Unterlagen prüfen. => We are supposed to check the documents.~Du musst nicht warten. => You do not have to wait.|An instruction reported with sollen is not the same grammatical meaning as the subject's own plan with wollen.
080|scheinen / beginnen / pflegen + zu-infinitive|Scheinen plus a zu-infinitive presents an appearance or inference, not a guaranteed fact. Literal scheinen, as with sunlight, does not require this construction.~Beginnen/anfangen identifies the start of an action. Pflegen plus a zu-infinitive describes a habit in a more formal register. Learn the placement of separable prefixes and zu; these modal-like groups are not ordinary modal verbs, so they keep zu.|Die Besprechung scheint lange zu dauern. => The meeting appears to be taking a long time.~Er pflegt früh aufzustehen. => He habitually gets up early.|Pflegen can also mean care for in other uses; identify whether an infinitive group follows it.
081|du solltest …; wir könnten …; ich würde …|Sollte gives advice rather than an uncompromising obligation. Könnte offers a possible course of action; würde plus infinitive makes a proposal or response tentative.~Match the form to the subject: solltest, sollten, könntest, könnten, würde, würden. An deiner Stelle provides a useful advice frame and fills the main clause's first position, so the finite verb follows immediately. The lexical verb stays a bare infinitive.|Du solltest eine Pause machen. => You should take a break.~An deiner Stelle würde ich nachfragen. => In your position, I would ask for clarification.|Distinguish past factual konnte from hypothetical könnte; an umlaut can change the entire meaning.
082|wenn … hätte/wäre/könnte, … würde + infinitive|Konjunktiv II presents an imagined or tentative situation. Use hätte for having, wäre for being and könnte for ability; many other actions use würde plus infinitive.~Both halves must fit the intended hypothetical meaning. The wenn-clause has final verb order; after a fronted condition, the main finite verb follows the comma. These forms can refer to the present or future despite their historical connection to past stems.|Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich öfter lesen. => If I had more time, I would read more often.~Wenn wir näher wohnten, könnten wir helfen. => If we lived nearer, we could help.|Do not interpret every hätte or wäre as past time; the presence of a participle is crucial for the later unreal-past pattern.
083|hätte/wäre gern; würde gern + infinitive; wenn … doch!|Use Konjunktiv II to describe a desired situation that is not simply being asserted. Hätte gern takes a possession/object; wäre gern a state or place; würde gern an action infinitive.~A wish can stand alone as Wenn ich doch mehr Zeit hätte! or Hätte ich doch mehr Zeit! The wish clause need not have a separate consequence. Person endings still matter, and gern is not another finite verb.|Ich wäre gern schon zu Hause. => I wish I were home already.~Wenn wir doch mehr Urlaub hätten! => If only we had more vacation time!|A verb-first wish with emphatic intonation is not necessarily a genuine yes/no question.
084|regular: stem + -te; strong: past stem; mixed: changed stem + -te|Regular simple past forms add -te and person endings; -t/-d stems often need an extra e. Strong verbs change their stem: finden → fand, schreiben → schrieb.~Mixed verbs combine a changed stem with weak endings: denken → dachte, bringen → brachte. Strong ich and er/sie/es usually share the bare past stem; plural forms add -en/-t/-en. Learn principal parts and read tense consistently within a narrative.|Der Zeuge sah den Unfall. => The witness saw the accident.~Er bemerkte den Fehler und rief an. => He noticed the error and called.|Präteritum is a single finite verb; do not add ge- as if you were forming a participle.
085|hatte/war + past participle = before a past reference point|Plusquamperfekt places one event before another already-past reference event. Conjugate haben or sein in simple past and retain the lexical verb's normal perfect auxiliary choice.~Use hatte for most transitive actions, and war for verbs such as gehen or ankommen. The participle closes an ordinary main clause; in a subordinate clause it comes before the final auxiliary. Temporal context, not merely the word gestern, motivates this earlier-past perspective.|Als wir ankamen, hatte der Film begonnen. => When we arrived, the film had started.~Sie war schon gegangen, als ich anrief. => She had already left when I called.|Hatte/war states an earlier past fact; hätte/wäre plus participle describes an imagined past alternative.
086|werden + bare infinitive = Futur I|Conjugate werden: werde, wirst, wird, werden, werdet, werden. Put the lexical infinitive at the end of an ordinary main clause.~Futur I can make a future plan or prediction explicit; German also commonly uses present tense with a future time expression. Distinguish lexical werden plus a state, meaning become, and passive werden plus a participle. Those constructions do not have the same grammatical structure as Futur I.|Wir werden nächste Woche umziehen. => We will move next week.~Sie wird die Rechnung morgen bezahlen. => She will pay the bill tomorrow.|Check the final verb: reparieren suggests future active, while repariert with werden suggests passive.
087|als: one past occasion; wenn: recurrence or present/future|Als introduces one past event or life period, even if that period lasted years. Wenn introduces repeated occasions, including repeated past habits, and realistic present/future conditions or occasions.~Both are subordinating conjunctions with final finite verbs. Wann asks about a time and belongs to a different question construction. Decide from the time relationship, not just from the English word when.|Als ich ein Kind war, wohnte ich in Bonn. => When I was a child, I lived in Bonn.~Immer wenn ich dort war, besuchte ich sie. => Whenever I was there, I visited her.|A long past life period can still be one als-period; length does not automatically make it repeated.
088|main event happens before the bevor/ehe event|Bevor and the less frequent ehe introduce the event that comes later in the sequence. Their finite verb goes last, whatever the tense.~Both events can use present forms for a future or general sequence. Do not add a perfect tense mechanically because the English translation contains before. If the bevor-clause comes first, the main finite verb follows the comma as usual.|Lies alles, bevor du unterschreibst. => Read everything before you sign.~Bevor wir losfahren, tanken wir. => Before we leave, we fill up with fuel.|In the second example, filling up happens first, even though the departure clause is written first.
089|nachdem earlier event, later main event|Nachdem introduces the event completed before the main event. In a past narrative, Plusquamperfekt in the nachdem-clause commonly combines with Präteritum in the main clause.~For a completed action relevant to a present/future next step, Perfekt plus present can express the sequence. Keep the auxiliary last inside the subordinate clause and separate the clauses with a comma. The chronological relation matters more than a mechanical one-tense rule.|Nachdem sie gegessen hatte, ging sie hinaus. => After she had eaten, she went outside.~Nachdem ich bezahlt habe, gehe ich. => After I have paid, I will leave.|The nachdem event is earlier, unlike the event in a bevor-clause.
090|während + finite clause = while|Temporal während connects overlapping events. It introduces a subordinate clause, so its conjugated verb comes last; the main clause supplies the other simultaneous event.~Separate the clauses with a comma and use main-clause inversion when the während-clause comes first. Distinguish the preposition während plus a genitive noun phrase and the contrasting use meaning whereas. Context determines whether time overlap or opposition is central.|Während sie kocht, decke ich den Tisch. => While she cooks, I set the table.~Ich lese, während wir auf den Zug warten. => I read while we wait for the train.|A finite verb after während signals a clause; während des Essens is a noun phrase instead.
091|seit/seitdem + beginning event → continuing state|Seit or seitdem can introduce a finite clause identifying when a state began. Its conjugated verb goes last; the main clause describes what has continued from that point.~German commonly uses present tense for a situation continuing now. Distinguish seit plus a dative noun phrase from the conjunction, and the linking adverb seitdem meaning since then, which follows main-clause verb-second rules.|Seitdem ich hier wohne, fahre ich Rad. => Since I have lived here, I have cycled.~Sie ist umgezogen. Seitdem hat sie mehr Platz. => She moved. Since then she has had more space.|Check whether seitdem introduces a subordinate clause or occupies the first position of a main clause.
092|main interval, bis + ending event|Bis introduces the event or state that ends a period of waiting, staying or continuing. Its finite verb closes the subordinate clause.~Do not confuse until with before: Ich warte, bis … says the waiting lasts up to that event. A time expression such as bis Freitag is a prepositional/adverbial phrase, not a finite clause. A fronted bis-clause is followed by the main finite verb.|Ich warte, bis du fertig bist. => I will wait until you are finished.~Bis der Bus kommt, bleiben wir hier. => We will stay here until the bus comes.|The main action continues before the bis event; the clause does not say that the event is already complete.
093|sobald + trigger, immediate next action|Sobald means as soon as: the subordinate event triggers the main action immediately or at the first available moment. It is more specifically temporal than a general if-condition.~Use final verb order in the sobald-clause. If it comes first, follow the comma with the main finite verb. Present tense can express a future trigger; a perfect form can emphasize completion before the next step where the context requires it.|Sobald die Antwort da ist, rufe ich an. => As soon as the answer arrives, I will call.~Wir fahren los, sobald alle eingestiegen sind. => We will leave as soon as everyone has boarded.|Contrast bis, which marks how long an interval lasts, with sobald, which marks the next action's trigger.
094|solange + continuing situation|Solange means for as long as or while a situation continues. The main action or state holds throughout that period, rather than starting only at its end.~Put the finite verb last inside the solange-clause. Contrast bis, an ending boundary, and sobald, an immediate trigger. Solange can also express a condition understood to remain valid over time; context supplies the interval.|Solange es regnet, bleiben wir drinnen. => As long as it is raining, we will stay indoors.~Ich helfe dir, solange du mich brauchst. => I will help you for as long as you need me.|Do not turn solange into a deadline; its focus is the continuing overlap.
095|da + reason clause; finite verb last|Causal da presents a reason, often as known or background information. It is a subordinating conjunction, so its finite verb goes last.~A da-clause commonly precedes the main clause, although it can follow it. After a fronted reason, put the main finite verb immediately after the comma. Distinguish this conjunction from local da meaning there, and from result-linking deshalb with main-clause order.|Da der Laden geschlossen ist, kommen wir morgen. => As the shop is closed, we will come tomorrow.~Ich frage nach, da mir die Nummer fehlt. => I am asking because I do not have the number.|Da ich bin müde … is not the standard subordinate-clause pattern; use da ich müde bin …
096|obwohl + fact contrary to the expected outcome|Obwohl introduces a true or accepted circumstance that would normally lead to a different outcome. The main clause states what nevertheless happens.~Keep the finite verb last and use a comma. Obgleich and obschon are less frequent conjunctions with a similar concessive meaning. Contrast weil/da for an expected causal explanation. The conjunction does not itself negate either event; the surprise comes from their relationship.|Obwohl es regnet, gehen wir spazieren. => Although it is raining, we are going for a walk.~Sie hilft, obwohl sie wenig Zeit hat. => She helps although she has little time.|Do not replace obwohl with trotzdem while keeping the same word order; trotzdem introduces a main-clause pattern.
097|fact. Trotzdem + finite verb + subject …|Trotzdem links a main-clause result to a preceding circumstance that would normally suggest the opposite. It functions as a sentence constituent, not as the usual subordinating conjunction.~When trotzdem comes first, the finite verb follows immediately. It may also appear later: Wir gehen trotzdem. Contrast obwohl plus verb-final clause. Keep both facts intact; trotzdem means nevertheless rather than because of that.|Es ist kalt. Trotzdem öffnen wir das Fenster. => It is cold. Nevertheless we open the window.~Die Fahrt war lang. Sie war trotzdem schön. => The journey was long. It was enjoyable nevertheless.|Do not write Trotzdem wir öffnen … when you mean a normal result-linking main clause.
098|action, damit + subject + … + finite verb|Damit introduces an intended result or goal. It can name a different actor from the main clause, which is why it is useful where a shared-subject um … zu construction would not work.~Keep the finite verb last and separate the clauses with a comma. The purpose is intended, not automatically achieved. Damit can also be a da-compound meaning with it; in that use it is not a conjunction and does not send the verb to the end.|Ich schicke dir die Adresse, damit du uns findest. => I send you the address so that you can find us.~Wir sprechen leise, damit das Kind schlafen kann. => We speak quietly so the child can sleep.|A purpose with damit differs from a factual consequence with sodass.
099|main action, um + … + zu-infinitive|Um … zu expresses why someone performs an action: its intended purpose. In the usual learner construction, the infinitive's understood actor is the main subject.~Put objects and reflexive pronouns inside the group; insert zu into a separable verb. Use a comma before um. Choose damit with a full finite clause when a different actor needs to be named explicitly. The phrase states intention, not guaranteed success.|Ich spare, um ein Fahrrad zu kaufen. => I am saving to buy a bicycle.~Sie kommt früh, um sich anzumelden. => She comes early in order to register.|Do not add an explicit second subject inside an ordinary um … zu group.
100|falls + possible contingency|Falls means if or in case a possibility occurs. It often presents the condition as a contingency rather than the repeated temporal occasions expressed by wenn.~Its finite verb goes last. When the falls-clause comes first, the main finite verb follows the comma. Present tense can refer to a future possibility; no special future auxiliary is obligatory. The main clause states the response if that condition holds.|Falls der Kurs ausfällt, informieren wir Sie. => If the course is canceled, we will inform you.~Ich rufe an, falls ich später komme. => I will call if I am going to be late.|Falls does not report an indirect yes/no question; use ob for whether something is true.
101|nicht/kein X, sondern Y|Sondern replaces or corrects an element that has been negated: not X but Y. The corrected elements should have parallel grammatical functions and retain their required cases.~Aber can contrast two points without replacing a negated element. Sondern also appears in the separate additive frame nicht nur … sondern auch. As a coordinating conjunction it does not itself require verb-final order in a new finite clause.|Ich brauche keinen Stift, sondern Papier. => I need paper, not a pen.~Sie kommt nicht heute, sondern morgen. => She is coming tomorrow, not today.|A simple positive contrast such as klein, aber hell is not the same relation as correcting a negated alternative.
102|entweder X oder Y|Entweder … oder presents alternatives. The coordinated elements can be words, phrases or full clauses, but their structures should fit the shared sentence frame.~A governing preposition/case still applies to the appropriate alternatives. Do not add both options as though the frame meant both-and. Entweder can appear before a phrase or clause; choose a clear, parallel learner pattern rather than moving it arbitrarily between unrelated elements.|Wir fahren entweder mit dem Bus oder mit der Bahn. => We travel either by bus or by train.~Entweder du kommst heute oder ich besuche dich morgen. => Either you come today or I visit you tomorrow.|The frame presents alternatives; it does not by itself assert that both options are carried out.
103|sowohl X als auch Y|Sowohl … als auch includes two equally relevant elements. Keep them parallel and preserve the case required by their shared verb or preposition.~When two ordinary singular subjects are coordinated as a group, plural agreement is the natural model. Do not confuse the fixed second half als auch with comparative als or equality wie. The paired phrase itself does not introduce a subordinate clause or force a final verb.|Sie spricht sowohl Deutsch als auch Englisch. => She speaks both German and English.~Sowohl Anna als auch Paul helfen uns. => Both Anna and Paul help us.|Als auch is a fixed partner of sowohl; ordinary oder would change the relation to alternatives.
104|nicht nur X, sondern auch Y|This paired frame includes both elements but emphasizes adding the second. Nicht nur does not exclude the first element; it says the first is not the whole story.~Put a comma before sondern and keep the coordinated material parallel. Shared case rules still apply, as in nicht nur mir, sondern auch dir. A full finite clause retains its own normal order; the paired conjunction does not make it subordinate.|Sie hat nicht nur Erfahrung, sondern auch Geduld. => She has not only experience but also patience.~Er hat nicht nur angerufen, sondern auch geschrieben. => He not only called but also wrote.|Do not interpret nicht nur as a denial that X is true; both X and Y are included.
105|weder X noch Y|Weder … noch excludes both coordinated alternatives. In the ordinary pattern, an additional nicht is unnecessary and may distort the intended meaning.~Coordinate matching words, phrases or predicates, and keep the cases required by the surrounding construction. Both items are negative alternatives, unlike entweder … oder, which offers choices. With full clauses or complex subject combinations, retain a clear agreement pattern rather than treating noch as a subordinator.|Er hat weder Zeit noch Geld. => He has neither time nor money.~Sie hat weder angerufen noch geschrieben. => She neither called nor wrote.|Weder … noch does not mean one option is false but the other true; it rejects both.
106|zwar X, aber Y|Zwar concedes a first point, then aber introduces the contrast that limits its practical importance. Both statements can be true.~Zwar normally behaves as a particle/adverb inside the first main clause, often after the subject or finite verb. Aber coordinates the contrast and does not send the finite verb to the end. Keep a comma before aber and choose phrases or clauses that actually contrast.|Die Wohnung ist zwar klein, aber hell. => The apartment is small, admittedly, but bright.~Sie hat zwar wenig Zeit, aber sie hilft uns. => She has little time, but she does help us.|Unlike sondern, this frame does not require replacing a negated first element.
107|antecedent gender/number + nominative relative role|A relative pronoun gets gender and number from the noun it describes, but case from its job inside the relative clause. If it is the clause's subject, use der, die, das or plural die.~Put the relative clause's finite verb last and mark its boundaries with commas. The antecedent can have a different case in the main clause; this does not change the relative pronoun's own subject role.|Ich kenne den Mann, der hier wohnt. => I know the man who lives here.~Das sind die Bücher, die hier liegen. => These are the books that are lying here.|Do not copy den from den Mann if the relative pronoun itself performs the action and needs nominative der.
108|relative accusative: den / die / das / die|If the relative pronoun is the direct accusative object, use den for masculine singular, die for feminine/plural and das for neuter. The relative clause has another subject that performs the action.~Gender/number still come from the antecedent, but its main-clause case is irrelevant. Keep the finite verb at the end, including a final auxiliary in a perfect clause, and enclose an inserted relative clause with commas.|Der Mann, den ich kenne, wohnt hier. => The man whom I know lives here.~Das Buch, das du empfohlen hast, ist spannend. => The book you recommended is exciting.|First find the relative clause's subject; the pronoun may describe a person without being that clause's subject.
109|relative dative: dem / der / dem / denen|Use a dative relative when a verb such as helfen, danken, folgen or vertrauen requires dative. Masculine/neuter singular use dem, feminine der and plural denen.~The pronoun's gender and number follow the antecedent; its case follows the relative clause's verb. Place the finite verb last and mark the clause boundaries with commas. Plural denen is a special relative form, different from the article den.|Die Frau, der ich danke, ist hier. => The woman whom I thank is here.~Die Kinder, denen er hilft, lernen viel. => The children he helps learn a lot.|Do not choose accusative simply because the English translation looks like an ordinary object.
110|preposition + relative pronoun + … + final verb|Keep a required preposition immediately before its relative pronoun: die Firma, bei der … The preposition or verb frame supplies case, while the antecedent supplies gender and number.~For two-way place prepositions, decide location or destination inside the relative clause. A person-preposition relative uses the same case logic as a noun phrase. Do not leave the preposition stranded at the end as in some English patterns.|Das ist die Firma, bei der sie arbeitet. => That is the company where she works.~Der Bus, auf den wir warten, kommt bald. => The bus we are waiting for is coming soon.|The main clause's preposition does not automatically become the relative clause's preposition; analyze the two clauses separately.
111|dessen: masculine/neuter; deren: feminine/plural|Dessen and deren express a possessive relation inside a relative clause. Choose by the antecedent: masculine/neuter singular dessen; feminine singular or plural deren.~The following possessed noun has its own role inside the relative clause, but does not decide between dessen and deren. These genitive relative forms do not decline like ordinary possessive determiners and normally replace an article before the possessed noun. Keep the final verb and comma boundaries.|Der Mann, dessen Tochter hier lernt, wartet. => The man whose daughter studies here is waiting.~Die Frau, deren Auto kaputt ist, ruft an. => The woman whose car is broken calls.|Deren Auto is possible even though Auto is neuter; deren refers to the female owner.
112|one clause boundary → one finite verb group|A relative clause can be inserted inside a dass-clause or another subordinate clause. Track each clause's own subject, complements and finite verb instead of treating all final verbs as one group.~Use paired commas around the inserted clause. After it closes, resume the containing clause and finish its verb group. Coordinating two subordinate clauses is different from embedding one inside the other: each still needs a recoverable structure.|Ich weiß, dass der Kurs, den du empfiehlst, morgen beginnt. => I know that the course you recommend starts tomorrow.~Sie sagt, dass der Mann, der hier wohnt, ihr hilft. => She says that the man who lives here helps her.|When a sentence feels crowded, temporarily remove the inserted relative clause to find the containing clause's verb.
113|known information/pronouns tend to precede new full phrases|The middle field lies between the finite verb and the final verb part. Unstressed pronouns and familiar information often come early; new or expanded noun complements often come later.~For two objects, neutral patterns are dative before accusative for full nouns and accusative before dative for two unstressed pronouns. A pronoun normally precedes a full noun object. Time–cause–manner–place is a useful tendency, not a universal command overriding focus and context.|Morgen schicke ich ihm den Vertrag mit der Post. => Tomorrow I will send him the contract by post.~Ich schicke ihn ihm morgen. => I will send it to him tomorrow.|Word order can change with emphasis; distinguish a neutral tendency from a claim that every other order is ungrammatical.
114|nicht before the targeted constituent; broad negation near the predicate's end|For a corrective contrast, put nicht directly before the element you reject: nicht morgen, sondern heute. Moving it can change the negation's scope.~Broad event negation is often late in the middle field but before a final participle/infinitive or closely connected predicate complement. Adjective and prepositional predicates normally follow nicht: nicht müde, nicht im Büro. Kein instead negates many indefinite/article-free noun phrases.|Er hat den Brief nicht geschickt. => He did not send the letter.~Nicht Anna, sondern Paul hat angerufen. => Paul, not Anna, called.|Do not assume that moving nicht leaves the meaning unchanged; identify exactly which alternative is being rejected.
115|noun reference: personal pronoun; proposition reference: da-compound|Choose a pronoun that agrees with its noun antecedent in gender and number, then give it the case required by the new sentence. A grammatical masculine object may therefore be er/ihn rather than es.~A da-compound often refers to a preceding event or fact: darüber, darauf, dafür. Linking adverbs can carry the relation between sentences without replacing a noun. Keep the reference clear when several possible antecedents occur.|Der Vertrag ist fertig. Ich lese ihn. => The contract is ready. I am reading it.~Sie hat geholfen. Dafür danke ich ihr. => She helped. I thank her for that.|Dafür points to the help, while ihr points to the person; both references have different jobs.
116|shared subject/auxiliary may be omitted in coordination|Coordinated predicates can share a subject or auxiliary: sie hat gelesen und geschrieben. The omitted material must be recoverable and still fit the grammar of both parts.~Nouns and compound parts can also be shared, as in der rote und der blaue Stift or Hin- und Rückfahrt. Short answers omit a whole recoverable clause. Do not omit a required element if the remaining second part would need a different subject, auxiliary or case relationship.|Er kocht und räumt danach auf. => He cooks and tidies up afterward.~Sie hat die Unterlagen geprüft und verschickt. => She checked and sent the documents.|Reconstruct the omitted words mentally; if the reconstructed clause does not fit, the omission needs revision.
117|modal particle + context/intonation → speaker attitude|Modal particles often add an attitude rather than factual content. Mal can soften a request; doch or ja can present something as already known; denn can add interest or surprise to a question.~Halt/eben can signal resigned acceptance, and bloß can intensify a warning. Meanings depend on context and intonation; the same written word may also have a non-particle use, such as causal denn or a literal repetition count with mal. Learn useful whole utterances.|Könnten Sie bitte mal nachsehen? => Could you please have a quick look?~Das weißt du doch. => You already know that, as I am reminding you.|Do not translate particles word for word or add them indiscriminately to formal writing.
118|vielleicht / wahrscheinlich / offenbar / angeblich / bestimmt / tatsächlich|These adverbs indicate different kinds of stance. Vielleicht gives a possibility; wahrscheinlich a probability; bestimmt strong speaker confidence.~Offenbar marks an inference from apparent evidence. Angeblich attributes a claim to others without guaranteeing it. Tatsächlich confirms what is actually the case, often against an earlier expectation. Keep evidence, reporting and confidence distinct: a confident word alone does not prove a claim.|Angeblich fällt der Kurs aus. => The course is canceled, according to what others say.~Das Licht ist aus; offenbar ist niemand da. => The light is off; apparently nobody is there.|Angeblich is not a synonym of definitely; it distances the speaker from the reported claim.
119|report a statement with dass; a question with ob/W-word|In ordinary indicative reporting, introduce statements with dass and yes/no questions with ob. Keep the W-word for an information question and place the finite verb last.~Adjust ich/du to the reported speaker and listener, and preserve the original event's time/place reference when the reporting situation changes. Morgen said on Monday refers to Tuesday even if you report it later. German does not mechanically require English-style tense backshift in every report.|Anna sagt, dass sie später kommt. => Anna says that she is coming later.~Er fragt mich, ob ich Zeit habe. => He asks me whether I have time.|Track who originally said ich before choosing er, sie or ich in the report.
120|compound: modifiers + rightmost head noun|Read a long noun compound from its final noun to identify the basic kind of thing. That head normally supplies gender and plural behavior: die Bescheinigung → die Teilnahmebescheinigung.~Earlier parts narrow the meaning. Linking -s-, -n- or other elements join parts but do not determine gender. Break a long compound into meaningful chunks; its practical meaning may be established lexically rather than a literal sum of every part. Recognition is more important than inventing long compounds.|Der Haustürschlüssel fehlt. => The front-door key is missing.~Die Teilnahmebescheinigung liegt hier. => The certificate of participation is here.|Do not assign gender from the first noun part or treat a linking -s- as the whole compound's genitive ending.
